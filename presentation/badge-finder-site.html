The hardware

The badge in your hands

What it has

  • WiFi — full scan, ESP-NOW, SoftAP
  • 802.11mc FTM — time-of-flight ranging
  • BLE — raw advertise + scan
  • Accelerometer — step counting
  • 6 RGB LEDs + 320×240 display

What it doesn't

  • GPS — useless indoors anyway
  • Magnetometer — no compass
  • Gyroscope — can't track turning
  • UWB / AoA antennas — no AirTag tricks

It has no idea which way it is pointing.

What we can measure

A distance is only a ring

FTM gives the distance to B

But it's only a radius. B is anywhere on the ring.

We need a direction.

HOW THE MATH WORKS

two captures, one short walk

Stage 1 · first capture

Two circles, two answers

RSSI → rough distances dA, dB. The circles cross at two mirror points.

for each shared AP, with A at (0,0) and B at (d,0):
x = (d² + dA² − dB²) / 2d y = ±√(dA² − x²)

Stage 1 · geometry check

The chords hand us the A–B line

The chords are all parallel — 90.00°, every time.

Their midpoints lie on one line: the A–B line.

Stage 1 · the limit

We know the axis — not the direction

Both directions fit the data — toward B, or exactly away.

Stage 2 · second capture

Walk a few steps, measure again

Steps count the metres. Direction = “straight ahead”

Re-scan from A₂ solves B — up to a mirror. Ranges are mirror-blind: the spin (or “warmer”) picks the side.

the on-badge solver (walk direction = +x):
x = (d₁² − d₂² + s²) / 2s θ = atan2(√(d₁² − x²), x)

Errors & assumptions

What can go wrong

The error

30–50%
error in a single WiFi distance estimate

The fix: a rolling average. The noise changes every scan — the truth doesn't.

The assumptions

  • No bodies blocking — a person costs 10–20 dB
  • Only light obstruction between badges
  • You walk roughly straight
  • Hallway-scale, not an AirTag — the screen says “LEFT or RIGHT: walk one way, warmer = correct”
