Finding a friend with borrowed WiFi

Two ESP32 badges know how far apart they are (802.11mc FTM) but not which way — no compass, no gyro. The building's access points, visible to both badges, become free reference points. Two captures and a short walk turn a distance into a direction.

Honest numbers

  • RSSI → distance is off by a factor of two in practice, so the dashed rings are really thick annuli. But with 5–8 APs the system is over-determined, and multipath errors on different APs largely average out.
  • The parallel-chords fact is real geometry — every chord is exactly 90° to AB — but in the only frame the badge can build it is true by construction. Stage 1 alone recovers the axis you chose, not a real-world direction. The walk in stage 2 is what supplies the missing information.
  • Expected accuracy ±15–25°, versus ±29° for the body-shadow spin it augments.
  • The endgame: APs are static. Cache their solved positions once, and every later locate is a single scan matched against a known map — no walking, no spinning.
